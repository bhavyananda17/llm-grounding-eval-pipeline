Hallucination Detection Methods

Automated detection of hallucinations in large language model outputs has become a critical research area as these models are deployed in high-stakes applications where factual accuracy is essential. Unlike mitigation strategies that aim to prevent hallucinations during generation, detection methods focus on identifying hallucinated content after it has been produced. These approaches vary in their assumptions, computational requirements, and the types of hallucinations they are designed to catch.

One prominent family of detection methods relies on consistency checking. The core idea is that if a model is asked the same question multiple times with different phrasings or sampling temperatures, factual information should remain stable across responses while hallucinated content will vary. SelfConsistency decoding, for example, generates multiple samples and identifies claims that appear in only a subset of responses as potential hallucinations. Another variant, called问query-based consistency, reformulates the original question into several related queries and checks whether the model's answers across these reformulations are logically compatible.

Knowledge-grounded detection methods compare model outputs against external knowledge sources. These approaches retrieve relevant documents from a trusted knowledge base and use natural language inference models to determine whether the generated text is supported, contradicted, or unrelated to the retrieved evidence. FActScore decomposes long-form generations into atomic factual claims and verifies each claim individually against a knowledge source, providing a fine-grained hallucination score. Similar approaches use Wikipedia or domain-specific databases as verification sources, though their effectiveness is limited to domains where reliable knowledge bases exist.

Calibration-based detection methods assess whether a model's expressed confidence aligns with its actual accuracy. A well-calibrated model should be less confident about statements it is uncertain of, and more confident about statements it can verify. Techniques such as token probability analysis examine the likelihood assigned to each generated token, treating low-probability tokens as potential indicators of hallucinated content. Semantic uncertainty methods go further by clustering semantically equivalent outputs and measuring the entropy of the distribution across clusters, using high entropy as a signal that the model is uncertain about the factual content of its response.
