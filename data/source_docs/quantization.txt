Quantization for Large Language Models

Quantization is a model compression technique that reduces the numerical precision of a model's weights and activations, converting them from high-precision formats such as thirty-two-bit floating point to lower-precision formats such as eight-bit integers or even four-bit integers. The fundamental motivation is that many of the bits used to represent full-precision weights contribute minimally to the model's output quality, and reducing this precision can dramatically decrease memory requirements and computational cost while preserving most of the model's performance.

Post-training quantization applies quantization to a model after it has been fully trained, without requiring access to the original training data or process. The quantization parameters, specifically the scale and zero-point for mapping between the original and quantized ranges, are computed either globally across all weights or per-channel to account for different distributions of weights across different layers. Weight-only quantization, which only quantizes the model's stored weights while performing computations in higher precision during inference, has become particularly popular for large language models because it significantly reduces memory requirements without the complexity of quantizing activations.

Quantization-Aware Training simulates the effects of quantization during the training process, allowing the model to learn weights that are inherently robust to the precision reduction. By incorporating quantization operations into the forward pass during training, the model can adjust its parameters to compensate for the information lost through rounding. This approach typically produces higher-quality quantized models than post-training methods, especially at very low bit widths, but requires access to training data and additional computational cost during the training phase.

Several specialized quantization methods have been developed specifically for large language models. GPTQ uses second-order information from the Hessian matrix to determine the optimal quantization order for weights, achieving four-bit quantization with minimal quality degradation. AWQ, or Activation-Aware Weight Quantization, identifies which weights are most important by analyzing activation patterns during inference and preserves higher precision for those critical weights. GGML and GGUF formats provide efficient quantized representations that enable running models with billions of parameters on consumer hardware with limited memory, making large language models accessible to a much broader audience of developers and researchers.
