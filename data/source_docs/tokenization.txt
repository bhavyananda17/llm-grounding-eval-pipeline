Tokenization in Natural Language Processing

Tokenization is the process of converting raw text into a sequence of discrete units called tokens, which serve as the fundamental input representation for language models. The choice of tokenization method has a profound impact on a model's vocabulary size, its ability to handle rare or unseen words, and its overall efficiency in processing text. Early NLP systems relied on word-level tokenization, where each unique word corresponded to a single token, but this approach struggled with morphologically rich languages and produced extremely large vocabularies that were computationally expensive to manage.

Subword tokenization methods emerged as a solution to these limitations. Byte Pair Encoding, originally a data compression algorithm, was adapted for NLP by iteratively merging the most frequent pairs of characters or subwords in a training corpus. This process continues until a desired vocabulary size is reached, resulting in a vocabulary that contains common words as single tokens while splitting rare or complex words into meaningful subword units. For example, the word "unhappiness" might be tokenized as "un", "happi", and "ness", allowing the model to leverage its understanding of the prefix, root, and suffix independently.

WordPiece tokenization, used in models like BERT, takes a similar approach but uses a different merging criterion. Instead of selecting the pair with the highest frequency, WordPiece chooses the merge that maximizes the likelihood of the training data when the merged token is added to the vocabulary. This likelihood-based criterion produces slightly different tokenizations than BPE, though both methods achieve the same fundamental goal of balancing vocabulary size with coverage. SentencePiece extends these ideas by operating directly on the raw text without requiring pre-tokenization into words, making it particularly effective for multilingual models that must handle scripts with no explicit word boundaries, such as Chinese and Japanese.

The impact of tokenization extends beyond simple text splitting. The tokenization scheme determines how a model perceives the structure of language, influences its ability to generalize to new words, and affects downstream task performance. Inconsistent tokenization across different languages can introduce bias, where high-resource languages receive more granular tokenizations while low-resource languages are split into less meaningful units. Recent developments in tokenization research focus on creating more equitable tokenizers, improving handling of code-switching between languages, and developing tokenization-free approaches that operate directly on byte sequences.
